2 Illustrative threads

The three threads below are hand-written examples of the cascade the prompt is designed to produce — they are not logged model output. They show the intended reasoning for the three hardest discriminations: invoice vs budget, memo vs letter, and form vs handwritten when handwriting appears inside a printed form. Step to them in the player above (they are tagged Illustrative), or read them here.

Invoice vs. Budget — payment demand decides

v17.2 rules · qwen/qwen3.7-flash (illustrative)

A page of charges with a total at the bottom. 
pre-scan: no mail-client header, no masthead, no memo block.
Check 1 file_folder: no — the page is a printed table, not a folder label.
Check 2 handwritten: no.
Check 3 letter: no — no salutation or closing.
Check 4 memo: no — no MEMORANDUM header.
The discriminating question from the prompt: is there a *payment demand* — an amount due to be paid, with vendor and billing terms?
The table lists line items with a grand total and 'AMOUNT DUE' — that is a demand for payment.
The prompt explicitly routes financial/money-data tables with a payment demand to invoice ('a bill is a bill even when it is printed on a form').
Check 5 budget: no — budget is a plan of expenditures, not a demand for payment.
</scratchpad>
<label>invoice</label>

Memo vs. Letter — the closing line settles it

v17.2 rules · qwen/qwen3.7-flash (illustrative)

A short typed page that opens ‘Dear Dr. Barnes’ and closes ‘Sincerely’. 
pre-scan: no masthead, no memo header.
Check 3 letter: yes — a formal salutation ('Dear Dr. Barnes') followed by body text and a closing ('Sincerely') with a signature block.
Memos are the reverse: a MEMORANDUM header with TO/FROM/DATE/RE lines, no salutation, no closing signature. This page has none of those markers.
The salutation + closing are strong, concrete, readable evidence, so I commit at Check 3.
</scratchpad>
<label>letter</label>

Form beats subject matter — a data page can still be a form

v17.2 rules · qwen/qwen3.7-flash (illustrative)

A page of technical values filled in by hand. 
pre-scan: the page has printed labeled fields ('NAME:', 'ACCOUNT NO:', 'DATE:') with values entered in the blanks — a structured form.
Check 2 handwritten: there IS handwriting, but the handwriting is *inside* a printed form structure, not a freehand note. The prompt's rule: classify by dominant function — the form fields control the page, so the handwriting does not win the check.
Check 1 file_folder: no. Check 3 letter: no. Check 4 memo: no.
The labeled-field skeleton with blanks is the strongest concrete evidence on the page.
</scratchpad>
<label>form</label>
